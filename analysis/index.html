The cosine is not the recorded change

Drill timber × Saw timber is 0.772 (#13). Drill timber × Operate drill/driver is 0.510 (#387). Close gripper × Withdraw gripper is 0.696 (#44). Open gripper × Withdraw gripper is 0.690 (#50). A higher score is not the same contact state.

Recorded endpoint changes can also diverge: #3 is +27.5° and −2.9° at 0.897; #6 is +92.2° and +2.2° at 0.822; #20 is +49.3° and −5.5° at 0.742. These are stored 2D records. They are not world rotation, clearance, or a completed path.

Conclusion

Similar action names help us find moments worth comparing. At the joint, those comparisons lead us to ask how the member can approach, who holds it in place, and when that support can be removed.

Comparison #3 shows why the footage matters. V28’s Hold timber at joint and V21’s Position timber at joint have a name similarity of 0.897. The drawn member axes change by +27.5° and −2.9° from first to last frame. These are two-dimensional observations from their respective cameras. The allowable insertion angle remains a question for a physical test.

Next step · V29

Does a chamfer help a timber member seat?

In V29, a robot gripper holds the member while a person steadies it by hand and operates a power tool. Can a chamfer give the member more room to align on entry, and does the member stay in place after temporary support is removed?

How we will measure it

  1. Set up a controlled comparison. Use the same timber member and slot width and depth, with two interchangeable entrances. Keep the orientation, starting position, and push limit consistent. Test a straight approach and progressively larger starting angles on either side, with three trials per angle for each entrance.
  2. Measure insertion. Mark the intended seating depth. Record how many attempts reach the mark without a second alignment, and report the largest tested starting angle that seats in all three trials, by side.
  3. Measure movement after release. After each successful insertion, remove the temporary support without adding a fastener. Record whether the member stays seated and measure its displacement from a fixed reference after five seconds.

What remains unverified

The V29 frame documents who is handling the member. The two sections are proposed test pieces inspired by MOCCA’s study of insertion directions. Trials are pending. The allowable approach angle, movement after release, and the amount of support supplied by the hand and gripper in the original assembly are still unknown. Any measured result will apply first to the bench setup.
